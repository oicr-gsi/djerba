Research Use Only (RUO) Reporting Statement
This is an automated research-use-only (RUO) report, which follows the format of our reviewed clinical reports. RUO reports are released as is, without review by our clinical interpretation team, using default pipeline settings for generation of reported metrics. Reported variants (snvs, indels, structural variants, fusions) are not reviewed to identify potential false positives. Recipients may want to further assess identified variants by review of the variant calls and the underlying aligned sequence.
Copy number analysis with PURPLE results in generation of multiple solutions based on a range of gamma parameters (an indicator of window size for segmentation). For reporting purposes a default solution is utilized for copy number assessment, and purity and ploidy estimates. Recipients may want to review the PURPLE results to better assess the suitability of the default solution.

Analytical Performance Characteristics of the Validated Assay
Based on a minimum tumour purity of 30%, the sensitivity for SNVs and in/dels is 96% and 89%, respectively. The sensitivity for CNVs and RNA fusions is 100% and 32%, respectively. The limit of detection is 10% VAF for SNVs and 20% for in/dels. The limit of detection for MSI is cellularity ≥50%. For HRD, the sensitivity is 83% and the specificity is 90%. The lower limit of detection is ≥50% cellularity in FFPE samples and ≥30% cellularity in fresh frozen samples. The upper limit of detection is 115X mean coverage. For LOH, the sensitivity and specificity are both 100%. LOH is currently reported for autosomes; LOH on the X chromosome is not reported.
Although whole genome sequencing encompasses all genes in a specimen, this report is restricted to cancer genes defined by OncoKB as of the date the report is issued.
The test was validated for somatic variant detection and it does not detect germline variants. Therefore, we cannot rule out the possibility that a germline variant exists that was not detectable using these methodologies. This test does not replace the need for germline testing in the context of hereditary cancer suspicion.
Laboratory results are subject to approximately 0.5% error in any of the pre-analytical, analytical or post-analytical phases of the test (Bonini et al. 2002).
This test was developed and its performance characteristics determined by OICR Genomics. It has not been cleared or approved by the US Food and Drug Administration.
